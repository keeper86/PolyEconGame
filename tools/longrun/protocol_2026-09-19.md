# Protocol 2026-09-19 — buy-side fill rate: not-placed guard + ask rule

30-year `baseline` runs (seed 1001, 128 agents), all `results/` dirs local.

## 1. `smoothedFillRate` pinned at 300 % (fixed)

A bid that was dropped at collection (no free storage, deposits scaled below EPSILON) left
`lastEffectiveQty = 0` and `lastBought = 0`; `adjustBidPrice` scored that "no demand" as a perfect
fill (`rawFillRate = 1`), which normalises to `1 + INVENTORY_SMOOTHING_MAX_EXTRA = 3.0`. The EMA of a
constant is the constant, so the bid sat at 300 %, `fillRateFactor` returned `priceAdjustMaxDown`
every tick and drove the price to the floor — a human player who could not get a bid placed saw a
permanently depressed price that ignored manual edits.

Fix: `lastEffectiveQty === 0` now short-circuits (price frozen, `notPlaced = true`, diagnostics
cleared), and `smoothedFillRate` is reset when the target is met.

Run `baseline-30y-notplaced-fix`: all bands pass.

## 2. Constant ask (rejected)

Replacing the ask with the constant smoothing maximum (`baseRate × (1 + extra)` = 3 days) at every
inventory level — no deficit cap, only the free-storage cap in `validateAndPrepareBuyBid` — is
**destabilising**. Run `baseline-30y-const-ask`, killed at y7:

| metric (mean per year) | y0 | y3 | y6 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `priceLevelManufactured` | 46.6 | 2499 | 5611 |
| `priceLevelRaw` | 1.8 | 44.9 | 218.7 |
| `maintFillRate` | 0.53 | 0.05 | 0.13 |
| `avgFacilityCondition` | 0.997 | 0.741 | 0.871 |

Mechanism: a permanent 3×-consumption ask keeps the buyer in permanent excess demand with no
"fully filled → bid down" anchor, so the price level compounds with the cost floors. This is the same
lever recorded in `skill-pricing-inflation.md` (buy-side over-bidding ≈ the 12× inflation engine).

## 3. Ramp removed, deficit cap kept (kept)

`smoothedDemand = baseRate × (1 + extra)` (constant 3 days) is now the *maximum*, still capped by the
remaining deficit, replacing the old `1 + extra × (1 − fillRatio)` ramp:

```ts
baseRateConsumption        = storageTarget / inputBufferTargetTicks
totalShortfall             = min(storageTarget − inventory, baseRateConsumption × (1 + extra))
```

`TARGET_FILL_RATE` 0.85 → 1.0. The fill-rate normalisation (`buySmoothing = 1 + extra`) is unchanged,
so the target keeps its meaning on the normalised scale (neutral raw fill = `target / (1 + extra)`).

Equilibrium is now `f* = 1 − (1 + extra) / (inputBufferTargetTicks × target_realised)`, i.e. `≈ 0.91`
of the level target at `target = 1.0`, and only weakly target-dependent — the fill-rate target is now
an eagerness/price knob, the buffer level is not.

Run `baseline-30y-ramp-removed`: **all bands pass** (population ×1.145, no starvation, grocery
fill 0.964, condition 0.986, construction price 1.64).

Input buffers do grow (`maint*Buffer`, mean y21-30, old → new): steel 1 298 → 14 113,
electronics 344 → 2 780, plastic 1 533 → 14 045, with `maintSteadyStateDemand` unchanged
(5 507 → 5 439). Input availability also improves slightly (`ironSmelterInputCoal` 0.903 → 0.959).

**Cost: the nominal price level is ~2.7× the previous baseline** at y30 (manufactured 54.8 → 146.5,
refined 9.8 → 28.3, raw 1.9 → 5.4, services 14.1 → 43.0). `gdpAnnual` 18.2e9 → 57.3e9 (nominal).

## 4. Target experiment: 0.9 / 1.1 (inflationary amplitude)

`TARGET_FILL_RATE = 0.9`, `TARGET_FILL_RATE_SERVICES = 0.9`, `TARGET_SELL_THROUGH = 1.1`,
`TARGET_SELL_THROUGH_SERVICES = 0.9` (services sell must stay below their `sellSmoothing = 1`; 1.1 is
degenerate — `sellThroughFactor` returns < 1 at a full sell-through, so service prices could only
fall, and it broke two `groceryMarket` tests).

Run `baseline-30y-fill090-sell110-svc090`: **all bands pass**, population/condition/starvation
unchanged, buffers within 5 % of the previous run (`maintSteelBuffer` 14 113 → 13 152). But the
nominal price level is far higher and the gap widens, i.e. a larger-amplitude price oscillation, not
a level shift:

| year | `priceLevelManufactured` ref (1.0/1.2) | new (0.9/1.1/0.9) | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 15 | 114.6 | 175.1 | 1.53 |
| 18 | 269.7 | 526.7 | 1.95 |
| 22 (peak) | 713.4 | 2312.2 | 3.24 |
| 26 | 182.5 | 630.8 | 3.46 |
| 30 | 146.5 | 972.5 | 6.64 |

The reference peaks at y22 and relaxes back to ~146; the new run peaks 3.2× higher at y22 and is
still 972 (and decaying slowly) at y30. `gdpAnnual` tracks the same ratio (nominal), so real output
is unchanged — this is a price-stability regression.

**No band catches it**: the baseline bands are population, starvation, grocery fill, facility
condition and construction service price. A price-level (or price-amplitude) band would be needed to
gate this class of change.

Attribution: the *fill* target and the *sell* target push prices in opposite directions when lowered —
a lower fill target means the buyer reduces its bid over a wider fill range (deflationary), while a
lower sell target means the seller raises its price at a lower sell-through (inflationary). In the
0.9/1.1 run the fill target fell (deflationary) and the sell target fell (inflationary); the outcome
is 3-6× more expensive, so the sell side dominated, on top of the loss of damping from the removed
`(1 − f)` ramp.

## 5. Ramp restored, targets 0.9 / 1.2 / services 0.9

`(1 − fillRatio)` ramp back in (`smoothedDemand = baseRate × (1 + extra × (1 − f))`), with
`TARGET_FILL_RATE = TARGET_FILL_RATE_SERVICES = 0.9`, `TARGET_SELL_THROUGH = 1.2`,
`TARGET_SELL_THROUGH_SERVICES = 0.9`. Run `baseline-30y-ramp-fill090-sell120`: **all bands pass**.

Early behaviour matches the original baseline, but the price level then ratchets up and does not
relax (`priceLevelManufactured`, mean y21-30 / y30 value):

| run | fill target | ramp | mean y21-30 | y30 | relaxes? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `notplaced-fix` | 0.85 | yes | 95 | 55 | yes (peak y21 → 55) |
| `ramp-fill090-sell120` | 0.9 | yes | 608 | 398 | no |
| `ramp-removed` | 1.0 | no | 329 | 147 | partially (peak y22 713 → 147) |
| `fill090-sell110-svc090` | 0.9 | no | 1155 | 973 | no |

Year 1-10 means are identical across the first two (41.0 vs 42.5); the divergence starts around
y11. Input buffers track the fill target loosely (`maintSteelBuffer` mean y21-30: 1 298 at 0.85 →
1 679 at 0.9), i.e. a +29 % buffer for +6× price level.

**Conclusion — the fill target sits on a knife edge at the break-even.** The buyer's neutral raw
fill is `target / (1 + extra)` and the buffer-steady raw fill is `1 / (1 + extra(1 − f))`; these
cross at `target ≈ 1/k ≈ 0.87` (k = the realised-fill overshoot). Below it the buyer systematically
bids *down* (prices anchored, buffer near empty); above it the buyer systematically bids *up* and the
price level ratchets without relaxing. 0.85 is just below, 0.9 just above.

So there is a hard buffer-vs-price-stability trade at this mechanism: you cannot get a large input
buffer by raising the fill target without destabilising the price level, and removing the ramp to
decouple them removes the price damping instead.

**All four runs pass the bands** while their price levels differ by ~7× — the baseline band set has
no price-level metric, so this whole class of regression is invisible to CI.

## 6. All targets 0.9, no ramp — largest buffer, worst prices

`TARGET_FILL_RATE = TARGET_FILL_RATE_SERVICES = TARGET_SELL_THROUGH = TARGET_SELL_THROUGH_SERVICES = 0.9`,
ramp removed again. Run `baseline-30y-all090-noramp`: **all bands pass**, but this is the most
inflationary variant tested. Mean y21-30 / y30 `priceLevelManufactured`:

| run | fill | sell | ramp | mean y21-30 | y30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `notplaced-fix` | 0.85 | 1.2 | yes | 95 | 55 |
| `ramp-removed` | 1.0 | 1.2 | no | 329 | 147 |
| `ramp-fill090-sell120` | 0.9 | 1.2 | yes | 608 | 397 |
| `fill090-sell110-svc090` | 0.9 | 1.1 | no | 1155 | 973 |
| `all090-noramp` | 0.9 | 0.9 | no | **3415** | **1415** |

All three changes push the same way: raising the fill target (buyer bids up over more of the fill
range), lowering the sell target (seller raises at a lower sell-through), and removing the ramp
(removes the "fully filled → bid down" damping). Result: ~36× the 0.85 baseline price level, with
population / condition / starvation unaffected.

The fill target sets the input buffer almost independent of the price outcome in the no-ramp variants
(`maintSteelBuffer` 13.1-14.1 k for every no-ramp run), i.e. the buffer is bought at the cost of the
price level rather than traded against it.

**Normalised-target symmetry.** All four targets are now strictly inside their ranges
(0 < 0.9 < smoothing, smoothing = 3 buy-goods / 1 services-buy / 4 sell-goods / 1 sell-services), so
each controller can both raise and lower. The "fill < 1 < sell-through" intuition is not structural:
the two targets live on separate normalised scales (raw-neutral points 0.30 fill vs 0.225 sell-through
for goods), and the only hard requirement is `0 < target < smoothing` — which is exactly what broke
the earlier services sell-through of 1.1 (smoothing 1).

## 7. 0.86 / 1.2 / services 0.86, no ramp — buffer AND lowest prices

`TARGET_FILL_RATE = TARGET_FILL_RATE_SERVICES = TARGET_SELL_THROUGH_SERVICES = 0.86`,
`TARGET_SELL_THROUGH = 1.2`, ramp out. Run `baseline-30y-fill086-noramp`: **all bands pass**, and it
is the best variant on both axes at once.

| run | fill | sell | ramp | `maintSteelBuffer` (mean y21-30) | `priceLevelManufactured` mean y21-30 | y30 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `notplaced-fix` | 0.85 | 1.2 | yes | 1 298 | 95 | 55 |
| `ramp-removed` | 1.0 | 1.2 | no | 14 113 | 329 | 147 |
| `ramp-fill090-sell120` | 0.9 | 1.2 | yes | 1 679 | 608 | 397 |
| `all090-noramp` | 0.9 | 0.9 | no | 13 100 | 3 415 | 1 415 |
| **`fill086-noramp`** | **0.86** | **1.2** | **no** | **13 275** | **78** | **40** |

So: ~10× the input buffer of the original baseline **and** the lowest long-run price level of every
variant (~18 % below the original baseline over y21-30, 28 % below at y30). Population ×1.1463
(highest), condition 0.988, grocery fill 0.974.

Price trajectory: the peak is higher and earlier (342 at y19 vs 205 at y21 for the baseline), but it
overshoots to the downside and ends clearly lower (y28-30: 46.7 / 41.4 / 39.6 vs 56.0 / 57.6 / 54.8).
The buffer is stable at 11-15 k from y15 on.

This confirms the decoupling: with the deficit-capped constant ask, the fill target sets the price
level and the buffer is held by the deficit cap independent of the target — the fill target of 0.86
sits just below the ~0.87 break-even, which is the deflationary side.

## Open items

- Price level: ~3× higher than `baseline-30y-notplaced-fix`. If that is too much, the lever is the
  ask magnitude (`inventorySmoothingMaxExtra` / the `(1 + extra)` factor), not the deficit cap.
- 600-year validation not run (`baseline` collapse mode is the maintenance-price spiral).
- `series.csv` has no per-input buffer metric; buffer levels were read from `maint*Buffer`. A
  `--goodsFillRate` flag plus an input-buffer metric would make the target→buffer curve measurable.
- Services are unchanged (they already used the uncapped deficit) and keep `TARGET_FILL_RATE_SERVICES = 0.85`.
